raea 0.1.10 makes the app raea cloud on your own computer. It is the same dashboard, with projects that run here on
the AI you already use, in the system's own window on the Mac, Windows and Linux. A finished video is published from
its project. In the engine, `raea make` holds a 3D scene, kit or shot to its plan until every check passes, Solari's
light boils less and can be measured holding still, and a long 3D film that switches scenes renders whole with the
same light as each frame alone.

**Get the app at [raea.io](https://raea.io)** (a Mac with Apple silicon, Windows 10 or 11, or Linux on x86_64).

The engine alone:

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raea.io/install | sh
```

On Windows, in PowerShell: `irm https://raea.io/install.ps1 | iex`.

## What changed since 0.1.9

### In the app

**The app is laid out like raea cloud.** Its rail is Inbox, Projects, Agents, Workflows, Libraries and Fingerprints,
with your workspace above New, and Connections and Settings at the foot. Projects you start run on this computer, in a
raea cloud of its own, on the AI you already use: Claude Code unless you pick another in Settings → General. Cloud
workspaces sit in the same menu once you sign in. The first time it opens, the welcome and sign-in come first, then
your projects, agents, library and fingerprints from the earlier app move in. The files are cloned, not copied, and the
old folders are only read.

**In the system's own window.** The app now draws in the webview each system already has: WebKit on the Mac, WebView2
on Windows and WebKitGTK on Linux. Links open in your browser, Copy reaches the clipboard, the folder chooser is the
system's own on every system, and a `raea://` link brings the running app forward on Windows and Linux as on the Mac.

**Publishing is a project's delivery.** A workflow can end in a post: pick the accounts it posts to in its editor. When
a project delivers its video, it waits in Needs you at Publish, with the caption its agent wrote and the account it
goes to. Change the caption if you like, press Publish, and the project shows "Published" with the post's views once
the network answers. On a plan that doesn't publish, Publish says which plan does and the project stays where it was.
The separate Publishing page is gone.

**Ask a workflow to change.** In a workflow's editor, say what you want ("add a legal review before it posts") and
your AI changes the workflow. When it needs to know more, it asks first.

**Agents and chats are one place.** Each agent is listed with its conversations under it, the busiest first. Click an
agent to start a new chat with it, or its gear for its settings.

**The Inbox counts what you haven't seen.** Its number drops as cards come into view, and colour is kept for what needs
you: what's running, done or stopped is grey.

**Projects belong to clients.** As in raea cloud, a project is filed under its client, and the client's board gathers
its projects. Projects from the earlier app that sat inside another one join its client. In a terminal,
`raea project new NAME --client CLIENT` names one, and `raea project tag --client CLIENT` changes it.

**`raea show` waits in your Inbox.** What an agent shows you from a terminal session is a card waiting for you, with
its files, filed under its project's client. Your approval, or your notes and comments, go back to the session that
asked.

**Mark up a picture.** The editor's picture toolbar has Pen (P), Arrow (A) and Box (B) beside Comment, in red, yellow
or white, with Undo.

**A review link for work done here.** On Pro and Studio, a project in a workspace on this computer can hand out one of
raea's review links for what it delivered.

**Ideate's voice is your raea plan's.** Talking an idea through in Ideate uses your plan's voice minutes: 30 a month on
Pro, 300 on Studio, and no limit on Enterprise.

**Choose where your work is kept.** Settings → General → Where raea keeps your work: pick a folder on any drive, and from the next
start raea keeps your work there, once what's already there has been copied and counted. The old folder stays as it
was (#233).

**Light and dark.** The app follows your system, or stays light or dark: Settings → General → Theme.

### In the engine

**raea make.** `raea make status` holds a 3D scene, kit or shot written as `make` in a `brief.ts` to its plan: what's
missing, each gate (its renders and checks, and raea ask's questions), and whether it's done. An agent's turn can't
end until it is. Around it: `raea make plan` draws the floor plan and a grey-box blockout to review first, `raea make
refs` makes every object's reference in one style, kits land as a component set with a lineup, and shots come from
words or a path through the floor plan. A client's brief can be its rules (`make: cut({...})`), and each object and
part can name its planned detail, from `lod={0}` (the hero) to `lod={4}` (far away), checked against how big it is on
the frame.

**Solari's light boils less.** `<SolariLighting paths={N}>` starts N paths a pixel and merges them, which cuts the
crawling patches on sunlit floors and reflections in held interiors by nearly half. `firefly={6}` cuts back a bright
sample that stands far above its neighbours, and `overscan={0.05}` traces a little past the frame's edge so its border
lights like its middle (on the Mac, where it rides the denoiser). Both are off unless set.

**Light you can measure holding still.** `raea render --hold FRAME` draws one frame again and again, and
`raea light steady` says whether its light holds still, and if not, how it moves: settling, boiling, jitter or flicker.
`raea light check` runs it on every traced scene.

**A 3D film renders whole.** In 0.1.9 a long film that switched scenes under Solari could draw its later shots darker
than the same frames rendered alone: a part a scene had left stayed in the traced scene and kept shading the next one.
It leaves now, and a film rendered whole matches each frame alone.

**raea render and raea scene check more.**
- Words in the world that sit behind a part, so a viewer can't read them, are an error that names the part in front.
  Text3D is checked as RichText is.
- A part a `<Label>` points at is the shot's subject: cutting it off at the frame's edge is an error.
- A part left in the air touching nothing is an error in `raea scene lint`, unless it's marked `floating`.
- A render whose shader didn't compile ends on an error, where before the frame came out without that pass.
- `raea check`'s contrast reads a moving text where it is on its middle frame, not where it ends (#236).

**raea fix.** `raea fix "the error text"` looks a problem up before it's guessed at: what others found and did about
the same symptom.

**raea/story.** A story as a typed program instead of a document: a premise, the values at stake, a cast with wants
and needs, who knows what and when, and beats that each turn a value. It reads a story through twelve lenses (McKee,
the Pixar spine, Campbell, Storynomics and others, plus a launch lens for product films), samples new stories from a
seed, compiles to a `script.ts`, storylets, Ink or Yarn, and `raea check` holds a script's scenes to their beats.
Measured against plain prompts, it works best as the editor of a draft rather than as the language a first draft is
written in.

**New in raea render and raea media.**
- `raea render --props JSON|FILE.json` hands a composition its inputs (`getInputProps()`), and `calculateMetadata` on
  a `<Composition>` sets its size and length from them (#176).
- `raea render --scale S` draws a composition at S times its size: 0.5 for a quick look, 2 for a bigger master (#175).
- `raea media share FILE -o OUT.mp4` makes the copy to send: any player takes it, and it's held to a bitrate (#219).

**The engine as a Linux AppImage.** Each release also carries `raea-VERSION-linux-x86_64.AppImage` and its checksum:
make it executable and run it as `raea` (#226). The project has a [code of conduct](https://github.com/shinyobjectz/raea/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) too
(#228).

**Fixes.**
- On Windows, a long chat scrolled in jerks, on fast graphics cards too (#215). In the system's own window a chat of
  160 turns scrolls at the display's rate, with or without the graphics card.
- Two paint bugs, each held to what Chrome draws: a box behind a word with `z-index: -1` covered the word (#241), and
  a rounded rectangle's stroke could draw a bar across the page from its left edge at some widths (#240).
- An Opus file couldn't be read back on Linux and Windows (#242).
- A failing audio device printed its error on every callback, and on Linux the app's log reached 40 GB in two days.
  The error is said once, then how often it came back, and the app's log is kept to two files of 32 MB (#239).
- An `<R3F>` over another layer cut a box out of the layer under it. The layer shows around the scene now (#235).
- A WebM with alpha that another tool had remuxed drew opaque. Its alpha is read whatever the tag's case (#224).
- `<Mix lufs>` could land a decibel off its target with flat-topped peaks. It lands on the target (#220).
- `raea sounds get` of a second instrument into one folder replaced the first one's notes and licence. Each
  instrument keeps its own folder (#217).
- `raea media sheet $FILES` in zsh, which passes every name as one argument, failed with "File name too long" (#218).
- A song's bars are the same on every platform: a 12 ms difference between decoders moved them (#49).
- A 3D scene with thousands of parts re-sent every part each frame. Parts whose props didn't change are skipped:
  a 3000-part scene went from 103 to 78 ms a frame (#160).
- On Linux, signing in failed with "spawn /usr/bin/security ENOENT". The app keeps the sign-in in the desktop's
  keyring, or in a file only you can read where there is none (#216, #222).
- `staticFile` from a composition in `compositions/` finds the project's `assets/`.
- A composition with the word "import" in a comment above its imports failed before its first frame with "Can't find
  variable: raea". It renders.
- A still of a page (`raea render --frame N`, or `--at` that doesn't start at 0) drew its CSS animations from the
  wrong time, so every animation showed its first keyframe. Each now runs from the composition's start, or from its
  own `<Sequence>`'s, as in the video.

**Known**
- On Linux, raea needs a processor with AVX (most from 2011 on) and glibc 2.39 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13,
  Fedora 40 and later).
- On Windows, TokenSynth runs on the CPU, so each note takes a minute or more to make.
- A CSS transition that a style change starts depends on the frames before it, so a still of that frame shows it
  finished. Animate with `@keyframes` or `interpolate` for a still that matches the video.
- A seek while a film plays in the timeline can show one frame from before the seek (about 33 ms) before it jumps.
- Characters (the Character SDK) come in a later release.
- R3F's pointer input has no double click yet, and touches don't arrive as pointers.
- postprocessing's Outline, TextureEffect, DepthEffect and LUT1D, and TSL's OutlineNode, are skipped, with one
  warning that names them.
- On a long held shot of an interior, Solari's light can still crawl in a few patches, most along the frame's edge.
  `raea light steady` shows where; `<SolariLighting overscan={0.05}>` lights the edge as it lights the middle (Mac
  only, about 1.2 times the tracing).
- The Windows app and engine are unsigned, as before: Windows asks once whether to run them, and with Smart App
  Control on, the engine can't bundle a composition (#221).
